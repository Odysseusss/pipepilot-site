Guide 2

Raised face and flat face flanges.

The facing controls where the gasket is compressed. It also affects compatibility, bolting practice and the dimension carried through the joint.

Raised face (RF)

A raised-face flange concentrates gasket loading on the annular raised surface inside the bolt circle. Ring gaskets and spiral-wound gaskets are common examples, but the actual gasket must follow the service, specification and flange standard. The raised portion contributes to the flange’s face geometry and must be recognized when a dimension is taken to the mating face.

Flat face (FF)

A flat-face flange presents one continuous plane across the face. Full-face gaskets extend across the flange face and include bolt holes. Flat faces are commonly associated with equipment or materials where bending stresses from concentrating the bolt load could be harmful. Treat the flange facing as a joint requirement, not a cosmetic symbol choice.

Do not casually mix faces

Bolting a raised face directly to a flat face can impose loads the flat-face component was not intended to receive. Project specifications may require a matching flat face, a spacer or another engineered arrangement. Confirm the approved detail rather than correcting the mismatch in the field.

Gasket thickness belongs to the joint

The installed gasket thickness occupies real face-to-face distance. For a flange-to-flange assembly, record gasket treatment and thickness at the joint so the overall route and cut calculations agree. Pipe Pilot treats a spoken “one-sixteenth gasket” as joint metadata rather than silently dropping it from a voice route.
