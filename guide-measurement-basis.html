Guide 3

End-to-end, end-to-center and center-to-center.

A length is incomplete until its start and end datums are known. Measurement basis tells the drawing engine—and the fabricator—exactly where the number begins and stops.

End-to-end (E–E)

End-to-end is measured between two physical ends or faces. For a plain pipe piece it can correspond directly to the cut length. For an assembly it may span flange faces, fitting ends or other stated endpoints. Confirm what “end” means at each side of the dimension.

End-to-center (E–C)

End-to-center begins at a physical end or face and stops at the centerline intersection of a fitting. A pipe running from a flange face to a 90-degree elbow center is a common example. To calculate the straight pipe cut, subtract the relevant fitting or flange takeout from the measured span according to the joint arrangement.

Center-to-center (C–C)

Center-to-center runs between fitting centers. The straight pipe between two elbows is shorter than the C–C dimension because each elbow occupies part of the span. Subtract the takeout at both ends, then account for weld gaps, gasket thickness or other specified joint allowances.

BasisAnchorsTypical cut relationship
E–EEnd or face to end or faceMay equal the piece length when no fitting takeout lies inside the span
E–CEnd or face to fitting centerSubtract the center-to-end takeout at the fitting side
C–CFitting center to fitting centerSubtract takeout at both fitting ends

A simple relationship

For a center-to-center spool between two fittings: pipe cut = center-to-center dimension − first fitting takeout − second fitting takeout ± specified joint allowances. Use the actual catalog and line class values. A long-radius elbow, short-radius elbow, tee and flange do not share one universal takeout.
